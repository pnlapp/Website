00 Guide

How to review losing trades

"I'll try to be more careful next time" is not a review. It's a feeling, and feelings don't compound. A process does.

Short answer

Reviewing a losing trade means separating three questions: was the trade well-planned, was it well-executed, and did the outcome simply fall outside normal variance? Most traders skip straight to "I lost, therefore I did something wrong" — which is how good decisions with bad outcomes get punished the same as bad decisions, and nothing actually improves.

01 The process

Five questions, in order

  1. Did the setup match your written criteria? If not, the review is already over — this wasn't a strategy trade, it was an impulse, and the lesson is about discipline, not the setup itself.
  2. Was risk defined and respected before entry? Check whether the stop was where it was planned, and whether size matched the plan — not what would've felt better after the fact.
  3. What was the emotional state at entry and exit? Calm and planned, or rushed and reactive? This is the variable most reviews skip, and usually the one that explains the pattern across many trades, not just this one.
  4. Was this a process loss or a variance loss? A well-planned, well-executed trade can still lose — that's normal variance, not a mistake. Don't "fix" a strategy because of a single normal loss.
  5. What's the one specific change, if any? Not "trade better." Something checkable: "wait for the candle close before entering," or "no new trades within 15 minutes of a loss."

03 A real pattern

What this looks like on an actual account

Composite example, from PnL App usage patterns

Ten losing trades reviewed properly in one month: seven were clean losses within normal variance, on-plan and well-executed. Three were tagged "rushed" or "off-checklist." The seven taught the trader nothing new — the strategy was working. The three, reviewed honestly, pointed at the exact same fix: no new entries in the first ten minutes after the market open.
