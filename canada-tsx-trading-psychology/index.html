00 Market Guide

Canada: TSX trading psychology

The rules of good trading don't change by market. What changes is which rule gets tested first, and how often.

Short answer

The TSX is heavily weighted toward energy and materials, especially gold and other metals, which ties its overall direction closely to commodity prices in a way most broad US indices aren't, so it can diverge from US market sentiment on any given day.

01 Know the session

An energy and materials-heavy index tightly linked to commodity cycles

The TSX trades 9:30am to 4:00pm ET, largely overlapping with US market hours, but its sector composition means it often moves independently of the S&P 500 on any given day.

What tends to move it: Energy and materials (especially gold and base metals) carry unusually heavy weight compared to most developed-market indices, tying the TSX's direction closely to commodity prices rather than broad US market sentiment.

02 The mistakes specific to this market

Five ways discipline breaks here

  1. Conflating stock conviction with commodity conviction. A TSX energy or mining name can look like a great individual pick while it's really just tracking the price of oil or gold. Traders who don't separate those two sources of conviction hold or add to positions based on a stock story that was never really the reason the position was moving.
  2. Assuming TSX moves in lockstep with US indices. Because of the heavy resource weighting, the TSX can diverge meaningfully from the S&P 500 on days when commodities move against broad market sentiment. Trading it as a simple proxy for US markets misses that divergence.
  3. Ignoring gold as a sentiment indicator for miners. Gold price direction often explains TSX mining stock moves better than company-specific news does, and missing that link leads to misattributed theses.
  4. Underestimating currency impact on cross-border positions. CAD/USD moves affect the realized returns of any TSX position held by a US-based trader, adding a layer of risk that's easy to overlook when focused purely on the stock price.
  5. Trading small-cap resource names on thin liquidity. The TSX and TSX Venture list many smaller resource companies with limited daily volume, where normal position sizes for a large-cap can move the price significantly in a small-cap name.
03 What actually helps

A short checklist before you trade Canada

  • Check the relevant commodity price (oil for energy names, gold or copper for miners) before finalizing a thesis on a TSX resource stock.
  • If you're trading cross-border, track CAD/USD alongside the stock price, it affects your actual return.
  • Confirm average daily volume before sizing a position in any smaller-cap TSX or TSX Venture name.
