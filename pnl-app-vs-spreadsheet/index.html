PnL App vs a spreadsheet

Plenty of disciplined traders built their process in a spreadsheet first. The honest question isn't whether it can work, it's what it depends on to keep working.

Short answer

A spreadsheet is free, fully customizable, and can absolutely work for a trader who is already consistent about logging and reviewing every trade. Its limitation isn't a lack of features, it's that it's entirely passive: it only shows what you choose to enter, on the days you choose to enter it, with nothing built in to catch a risky decision as it's happening. PnL App automates the capture and adds real-time behavioral flags a static sheet simply can't provide.

01 Where they differ

A record vs. an active system

  1. A spreadsheet depends on the discipline it's meant to build. The traders who struggle most with consistency are often the same traders who stop updating a spreadsheet the moment things get emotionally difficult, which is exactly when the data would be most useful.
  2. Nothing happens automatically. A spreadsheet doesn't notice that size crept up after a loss or that a stop got moved twice this week. It only shows that if you specifically go looking for it.
  3. No real-time intervention is possible. A cell can't lock your broker app for twenty minutes after a loss. That kind of in-the-moment circuit breaker requires something built for it.
  4. A spreadsheet's real advantage is total control. For a trader who wants to design their own exact tracking system and is confident they'll maintain it, that flexibility is genuinely valuable, and building one for a month is a reasonable way to learn what a journal should even capture.
02 Who each one fits

Matching the tool to the actual problem

A practical way to decide

If you've maintained a spreadsheet consistently for months and it's working, there's no urgent reason to switch. If your spreadsheet has three weeks of entries followed by a gap, followed by another three weeks, that gap pattern is itself useful information, and it's usually a sign the tracking needs to require less willpower to keep up, not more.

“The best tracking system is the one you actually keep using after a bad week.”

Less friction, more intervention

A journal that acts, not just records.

PnL App captures trade context automatically and flags risky patterns in real time, so the tracking survives the exact weeks a spreadsheet usually gets abandoned.
